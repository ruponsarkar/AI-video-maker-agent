## Source: Indian Banking System — Complete Guide 2026 | Bankopedia
https://www.bankopedia.co.in/banking/indian-banking-system-complete-guide-2026

- Indian banking system manages total assets exceeding ₹250 lakh crore (approx USD 3 trillion) as of 2026  
- Serves over 1.5 billion people and operates through more than 1,50,000 branches and 2,50,000+ ATMs  
- RBI is the central bank established in 1935 under the Reserve Bank of India Act, 1934  
- Indian banking system has a two-tier structure: Tier 1 (RBI) and Tier 2 (commercial and cooperative banks)  
- RBI regulates, supervises, and manages foreign exchange reserves of all banks  
- Banks in India accept deposits, extend credit, and provide payment services to individuals, businesses, and the government  
- The system facilitates payments through NEFT, RTGS, IMPS, UPI, and other payment infrastructure  
- RBI’s monetary policy decisions (like repo rate changes) influence the broader economy via the banking system  
- Banks act as intermediaries by channeling savings into productive investments  
- Deposit insurance is provided to protect depositors' funds in case of bank failure  
- If all customers withdraw money at once, banks can face liquidity crisis but have mechanisms to manage it  
- RBI ensures stability and growth through regulatory frameworks and monetary transmission  
- The system supports economic development by enabling credit flow to businesses and individuals

## Source: Banking regulation in India: overview - AZB & Partners
https://www.azbpartners.com/bank/banking-regulation-in-india-overview/

- RBI (Reserve Bank of India) is the main regulator of banking in India  
- Banking Regulation Act, 1949 governs banking business and financial services  
- RBI Act, 1934 gives RBI power to set rules, regulations, directions, and guidelines for banks  
- Foreign Exchange Management Act, 1999 regulates cross-border transactions and foreign exchange activities  
- RBI sets monetary policy, regulates money, foreign exchange, government securities markets, and financial derivatives  
- RBI oversees payment and settlement systems and manages currency  
- SEBI regulates the securities market in India  
- IRDAI regulates the insurance sector  
- IBBI handles insolvency and bankruptcy processes under IBC  
- Central government (via Ministry of Finance) supervises and legislates on bank operations  
- Public sector banks follow norms set by Department of Financial Services  
- Banks need RBI license to operate, and can also do ancillary activities like trade finance, financial leasing, hire purchase, etc.  
- Entities dealing in foreign exchange must get a separate authorized dealer license from RBI
