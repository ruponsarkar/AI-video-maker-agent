## Source: Why Are Fireworks So Loud: The Science Behind The Boom
https://www.redapplefireworks.com/blogs/we-love-fireworks/why-are-fireworks-so-loud

- Fireworks are loud because of rapid combustion that creates shockwaves  
- Black powder (potassium nitrate, charcoal, sulfur) burns quickly, producing hot gas and expanding rapidly  
- Sudden gas expansion pushes air, creating high-pressure front (shockwave) which we hear as a boom  
- Faster and more confined combustion makes the shockwave sharper and louder  
- Lift charge creates the initial "thump" by forcing the shell upward with expanding gas  
- Burst charge at altitude breaks open the shell and spreads stars for color effects  
- Larger shells use more lift powder, creating more expanding gas and louder launch sound  
- Tight confinement of flash powder in salutes makes them very loud and sharp  
- Salute fireworks focus on loud report instead of visual effects using flash powder and tight design  
- Multi-shot cakes spread noise over smaller bursts, making the overall experience less intense

## Source: Noise pollution - Wikipedia
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noise_pollution

- Diwali ke patakhe ki tez awaz banane mein gas ka rapid expansion aur pressure ka use hota hai  
- Sound waves create when gas expands rapidly, causing vibrations in the air  
- Shock waves form due to sudden pressure changes during firecracker explosion  
- Noise pollution is caused by loud sounds like explosions, generators, and traffic  
- High noise levels can harm health, including heart problems and hearing loss  
- Sound pressure level (SPL) measures the force of sound waves in decibels (dB)  
- Repeated exposure to over 85 dB can damage hearing  
- Noise assessment uses metrics like L_eq and L_dn to measure long-term exposure  
- Frequency of sound is measured in hertz (Hz), showing how many waves pass per second  
- Humans hear between 20 Hz to 20,000 Hz, but sensitivity decreases with age
