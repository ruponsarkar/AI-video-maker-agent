## Source: How Do Fireworks Get Their Colours? The Complete Science Guide
https://www.galacticfireworks.co.uk/blogs/learn-about-fireworks/the-science-behind-fireworks-how-different-chemicals-create-explosive-colours

- Fireworks get their colours from metal salts burned in small pellets called stars  
- Different metals emit different wavelengths of light: strontium (red), barium (green), copper (blue), sodium (yellow)  
- The colour depends on the metal used and how hot the star burns  
- Stars are pea-sized pellets made of metal salts, oxidisers, binder (dextrin), and fuel (charcoal)  
- Firework colours come from the stars burning in mid-air after the burst charge detonates  
- Strontium salts (SrCO₃ or Sr(NO₃)₂) make red, emitting light at 650-700 nm  
- Barium compounds (Ba(ClO₃)₂ or Ba(NO₃)₂) make green, emitting at 505-535 nm  
- Copper(I) chloride (CuCl) makes blue, emitting at 440-480 nm  
- Sodium contamination can ruin barium's green by overpowering it with yellow  
- Blue is hard to produce because copper chloride decomposes above 1,200°C  
- Firework stars burn at 1,500-2,000°C, requiring precise temperature control for blue  
- Copper(I) iodide is slightly more heat-stable than copper chloride but still challenging  
- Red is the easiest and cheapest colour to produce due to stable strontium salts  
- Temperature, fuel-to-oxidiser ratio, and contamination affect the final colour quality

## Source: Science behind diwali fireworks | Obsessed With Science
https://obssessedwithscience.wordpress.com/2014/10/23/science-behind-diwali-fireworks/

- Fireworks produce colorful lights due to metal salts like strontium carbonate (red), calcium chloride (orange), sodium nitrate (yellow), barium chloride (green), copper chloride (blue), and mixtures for purple.  
- Metal salts are packed into pellets called "stars" inside the firework shell.  
- When ignited, heat excites electrons in metal atoms, which then release light as they return to their ground state.  
- Different colors are created by different metal salts: red from strontium, orange from calcium chloride, yellow from sodium nitrate, green from barium chloride, blue from copper chloride.  
- Purple fireworks use a mix of strontium (red) and copper (blue).  
- Gold color comes from a combination of carbon, sulfur, and potassium nitrate.  
- Sparklers contain oxidizers like potassium nitrate or chlorate, fuel like sugar or starch, and metal powders like aluminum or iron to create sparks.  
- The binder in sparklers is often sugar, starch, or shellac.  
- Metal flakes in sparklers heat up and glow brightly or burn at high temperatures.  
- Rocket engines use black powder-type fuel.
